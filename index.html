The Solution

✅ Agile Sprint System

Structured, time-boxed, collaborative delivery

What is Agile?

A structured approach where teams deliver value in fixed time cycles with clear planning, collaboration, and continuous improvement.

  • → Fixed 2-week work cycles (Sprints)
  • → Clear commitments & goals
  • → Daily sync — not daily assignment
  • → Team self-organization

What is a Sprint?

A 2-week time-boxed period where teams commit to completing specific work with clear goals and deliverables.

  • → 2 weeks = 1 Sprint cycle
  • → Scope locked at sprint start
  • → Working deliverables at end
  • → Review & improve after every cycle

Work Structure

📊 Work Hierarchy

From big picture to atomic tasks

🎪

EPIC

Large initiative — Big picture goal spanning multiple sprints

Example: "Sales Custom Module Development" or "New Payment System"
↓
📝

STORY

User-focused unit — Specific value delivered to the user

Example: "As a customer, I want to pay via UPI"
↓
⚡

FEATURE

Specific functionality — A deliverable component of a story

Example: "UPI Payment Gateway Integration"
↓
✅

TASK

Specific action — Individual work item assigned to a person

Example: "Create API endpoint", "Fix login bug"
Important Categories:
  • Small Jobs → Separate TASKS
  • Bugs → Separate TASKS with priority labels
  • Separate Spaces: Tech, AI & Design each have their own JIRA spaces

Transparency Protocol

📢 Product Team Communication

Information shared with Product Team / EPIC & Task Owners across key events

💡

Why This Matters

Keeping EPIC owners and the product team informed at the right moment eliminates surprises, builds trust, and ensures sprint commitments are met with full stakeholder visibility. No more information gaps.

1

📋 Planned Sprint Task List

On the very first day of each sprint, share the complete planned task list including all items committed to for the sprint, assigned owners, story point estimates, and the sprint goal. Product team and EPIC owners receive full visibility from Day 1.

🗓️ Sprint Day 1 📧 Via Email / JIRA 👥 Product Team 📌 EPIC Owners
2

🔀 Mid-Sprint Changes

Any changes during the sprint — new tasks added, existing tasks modified, or items deprioritized — must be communicated immediately after approval (or with approval over email). No silent scope changes. Full transparency on sprint impact.

⚡ On Change ✅ Post Approval 📩 Email Trail 🔁 Impact Analysis
3

⚠️ Spillovers, Delays & Blockers

Early identification and immediate communication of any risks — items likely to spill into next sprint, delays in delivery, or blockers preventing progress. Proactive alerts, not post-mortems. EPIC owners informed as soon as risk is identified.

🚨 Immediate Alert 🔴 Blocker Flagged 📊 Impact Shared 🛠️ Resolution Plan
4

🚀 Sprint Release

When sprint deliverables are released — whether mid-sprint priority release or end-of-sprint release — product team and EPIC owners receive a release summary covering what was shipped, what's live, and any known issues or follow-ups.

🎉 On Release 📦 What Shipped 🔗 Release Notes 🐛 Known Issues
5

📌 Miscellaneous Updates

Any other relevant information — team bandwidth changes, dependency alerts, tool or process updates, sprint velocity insights, or context useful for planning future sprints. Keeps product team informed of factors affecting delivery.

📉 Velocity Trends 🔗 Dependencies 👥 Team Changes 🛠️ Process Updates

  • →Product team notified immediately on release

📊 Reports & Insights

  • →Track taken up vs delivered each sprint
  • →Monitor velocity and capacity trends

  • →Analyze blockers and bottlenecks

  • →Assign and separate issues by owner
  • →Know exactly which person is responsible

If something urgent arrives mid-sprint, it CAN be allocated — but with transparent impact analysis shared with the product team and EPIC owners. No more invisible scope creep.
