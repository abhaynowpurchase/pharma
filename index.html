Definition of Pharmacology

Pharmacology is the science that deals with the study of drugs and their interactions with living systems, including their effects, mechanisms of action, therapeutic uses, and adverse effects.

Key Aspects

  • Study of drug composition and properties
  • Effects of drugs on biological systems
  • Therapeutic applications
  • Drug interactions and adverse effects
Slide 2

Branches of Pharmacology

  • Pharmacokinetics: Study of drug absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion
  • Pharmacodynamics: Study of drug mechanisms and effects on the body
  • Pharmacotherapeutics: Use of drugs in treatment of diseases
  • Toxicology: Study of harmful effects of substances
  • Clinical Pharmacology: Application of pharmacology in clinical settings
  • Pharmacy: Preparation and dispensing of drugs
  • Pharmacognosy: Study of drugs from natural sources
  • Posology: Study of drug dosages

Key Definitions (Part 1)

Pharmacology

The science of drugs including their origin, composition, properties, effects, and therapeutic uses.

Pharmacokinetics

What the body does to the drug - study of ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion).

Pharmacodynamics

What the drug does to the body - study of drug action, mechanisms, and effects.

Slide 4

Key Definitions (Part 2)

Pharmacotherapeutics

The branch dealing with the use of drugs in the prevention and treatment of diseases.

Clinical Pharmacology

The study of drugs in humans, focusing on safe and effective therapeutic use in patients.

Toxicology

The study of poisons and adverse effects of drugs, including detection, effects, and treatment of poisoning.

What is a Drug?

Definition

A drug is any substance that, when administered to a living organism, produces a biological response and is used for diagnosis, prevention, treatment, or cure of disease.

Examples of Drugs

  • Aspirin (analgesic)
  • Insulin (antidiabetic)
  • Penicillin (antibiotic)
  • Morphine (analgesic)
  • Digoxin (cardiac glycoside)
  • Paracetamol (antipyretic)
  • Atenolol (antihypertensive)
  • Omeprazole (antiulcer)

Sources of Drugs

1. Plant Sources

Examples: Morphine (Papaver somniferum), Digoxin (Digitalis purpurea), Atropine (Atropa belladonna)

2. Animal Sources

Examples: Insulin (pancreas), Heparin (intestinal mucosa), Thyroid hormones

3. Mineral Sources

Examples: Iron, Calcium, Magnesium sulfate, Potassium permanganate

4. Microbial Sources

Examples: Penicillin (Penicillium), Streptomycin (Streptomyces)

5. Synthetic Sources

Examples: Aspirin, Paracetamol, Sulfonamides

6. Biotechnology

Examples: Recombinant insulin, Monoclonal antibodies, Vaccines

Slide 7

Drug Nomenclature

1. Chemical Name

Scientific name based on chemical structure

Example: N-(4-hydroxyphenyl)acetamide

2. Non-proprietary / Generic Name

Official name assigned by regulatory authorities (WHO, USAN)

Example: Paracetamol / Acetaminophen

3. Proprietary / Brand Name

Trademark name given by the pharmaceutical company

Example: Crocin, Tylenol, Calpol

Sources of Drug Information

Primary Sources

  • Original research articles in peer-reviewed journals
  • Clinical trial reports

Secondary Sources

  • Pharmacopoeias: IP (Indian), BP (British), USP (United States)
  • Textbooks: Goodman & Gilman, Rang & Dale
  • Drug formularies: National Formulary, BNF

Tertiary Sources

  • Drug information databases (PubMed, Medline)
  • Package inserts and drug labels
  • Online resources (WHO, FDA websites)

Parts of a Prescription

1. Superscription (℞): Symbol meaning "take thou" or "recipe"
2. Inscription: Name and quantity of drug(s) prescribed
3. Subscription: Directions to the pharmacist for dispensing
4. Signatura (Sig.): Directions to the patient for taking the medicine
5. Prescriber's Details: Name, qualification, registration number, signature, date
6. Patient's Details: Name, age, sex, address
